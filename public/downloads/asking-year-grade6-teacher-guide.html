Teacher Guide

The Asking Year · Grade 6 · 36 weeks

Answers, the point of each week, and the specific wrong turn each one produces. Written for a teacher who has never run an enterprise program and does not intend to become an expert in one.

Where a question is a judgment call this guide says so rather than inventing a correct answer to mark against. A student’s own plan is the clearest case: what can be marked is whether it adds up, and nothing else about it can.

No page asks a child what anything costs at their house. Every amount in the book is either the student’s own or is printed on the page. Some children in the room will be in homes where money is a hard subject, and the year is built so that they never have to say so to finish it.

All 314 numeric answers below are recomputed from the problem as stated in the workbook by verify.py, which ships beside this file and runs on every build.

Trimester 1 · Weeks 1 to 12

Percent, and what it is doing

Weeks 1 to 4 are percent done properly, because a student who cannot find twenty percent of a price cannot check anything at all. Week 5 is the move: a percent off is a fact about a percent AND a price, and only one of them is on the sign. Week 6 is 'up to', a ceiling with no floor. Weeks 7 to 9 are framing: the same money two ways, the first number as a ruler, and the nine at the end of a price.

The point

Students who find 20 percent by dividing by 20. It works for exactly one percent and fails everywhere else; ask them to try it on 15 percent.

Trimester 2 · Weeks 13 to 24

Numbers that go both ways

A balance below zero, which is where a sixth grader meets a negative number and immediately knows what it means, and the flat fee that falls hardest on whoever was least short. Then the spread: one price is a sample, mean and median answer different questions, and one far value moves one of them. Weeks 19 to 23 finish with unit price, the bigger pack that costs more per unit, FREE, and who is paid to ask you.

The strands

PercentA rate out of a hundred, worked out rather than accepted, and always a rate OF something. The second half is the year.
FramingThe same money presented two ways, the first number as a ruler for every number after it, and the nine at the end of a price.
Below ZeroA balance less than nothing, which is a position rather than an error, and what it costs to be there.
SpreadOne price is a sample. Mean and median both name a middle and answer different questions, and one far value moves only one.
ComparingUnit price at Grade 6 precision, and the cases where the bigger pack and the bigger percent are not the better buy.
RulesA cost written with a letter in it, so one line covers every number of them, and an inequality for how many you can have.
AskingTurning a claim back into a question somebody can answer with a fact rather than with enthusiasm.
The LimitsWhere checking runs out: a claim nobody could verify, and the decision you have to make anyway.
ReviewPulling a trimester into something a student could act on.

Four things this book does not do

Nothing is sold. There is no product, no customer and no market day anywhere in this year. That is The Market Year, which is also Grade 6 and is a different book in a different folder. It teaches percent from the seller’s side; this teaches it from the side of the person being sold to.

Nobody is asked what anything costs at their house. Every amount is the student’s own or is printed on the page. If a child volunteers a family amount, do not write it on the board, do not compare it with anybody else’s, and move back to the page.

No decision is marked right or wrong. A student's own agreement, their own shares, their own old purchase costed honestly: none of those has a right answer. What can be marked is the arithmetic inside them, and there is plenty of it: whether the shares reach one whole, whether the amounts add back to the cost, whether the cost per use was divided the right way round. Whether a thing was worth buying is not a question this guide answers, and a teacher who answers it will get pages written to please them rather than pages that are true.

Nobody is charged for lending. Weeks 18 to 21 are about money owed between friends, and none of it earns anything. Nobody in this book pays back more than they borrowed, and week 21's challenge asks a student what they would do if somebody offered. Interest is Grade 8 in the pathway and it stays there.
