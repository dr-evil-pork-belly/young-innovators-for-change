What Goes Together

Grade 1 teacher guide. All 36 weeks, with the words to say out loud, every answer, and the wrong turn each week produces.

Read the say-this line out loud. The student page carries exactly one sentence, because in September most first graders cannot read more than that. The sentence on the page and the line in this guide are not the same words: the page is written to be decoded, this one is written to be heard.

Every number below is recomputed from the problem as the workbook states it by verify.py, which ships with the source and runs on every build.

Week 1. One Pile or the Other

Sorting & Rules · In class: Sorting and describing objects

Say thisLook at all the shapes. Some are round and some are not. Put every single one in a hoop.

The first act of the year: hold a rule in your head and use it. Nobody is asked what the rule is, because that is Grade 2 week 1.

On the page

Put every shape in one pile or the other.

Answers

Round shapes3
Blue shapes3
Shapes in all8
Left over0
Watch for. Children who invent a third pile for the ones they are unsure about. The rule has exactly two answers, and saying so is the lesson.
If it stalls. Hand them one shape and ask only about that shape. Then the next one.

Week 2. What Is the Same?

Sorting & Rules · In class: Comparing objects

Say thisLook at these two. Say one way they are the same.

Naming a shared attribute out loud, in a sentence. The sentence is the work.

On the page

Say one way these two are the same.

Watch for. One-word answers. 'Blue' is not yet an answer; 'they are both blue' is.
If it stalls. Give them the sentence frame and let them fill the end: they are both ___.

Week 3. What Is Different?

Sorting & Rules · In class: Comparing objects

Say thisLook at the same two. Now say one way they are different.

Same and different are one skill, asked from two sides.

On the page

Say one way these two are different.

Watch for. Children who repeat the sameness answer with a 'not' in front of it.
If it stalls. Point at one, then the other, and ask what changed between your fingers.
