Teacher Guide

The Planning Year · Grade 4 · 36 weeks

Answers, the point of each week, and the specific wrong turn each one produces. Written for a teacher who has never run an enterprise program and does not intend to become an expert in one.

Where a question is a judgment call this guide says so rather than inventing a correct answer to mark against. A student’s own plan is the clearest case: what can be marked is whether it adds up, and nothing else about it can.

No page asks a child what anything costs at their house. Every amount in the book is either the student’s own or is printed on the page. Some children in the room will be in homes where money is a hard subject, and the year is built so that they never have to say so to finish it.

All 233 numeric answers below are recomputed from the problem as stated in the workbook by verify.py, which ships beside this file and runs on every build.

Trimester 1 · Weeks 1 to 12

To the cent, and the first plan

Weeks 1 to 4 stop rounding: a dollar is a hundred parts, $4.09 and $4.90 are not close, and money is added by lining the points up. Week 5 is the move. Weeks 6 to 8 build the first plan and check that its parts reach the whole. Weeks 9 to 11 compare at the new precision, and week 11 is the one that matters: cheaper per one is not cheaper if you did not want six.

Trimester 2 · Weeks 13 to 24

Keeping it

The plan column, then the actual column beside it, then the difference, then the week a category runs out on Thursday. Weeks 17 to 19 give the plan fractions, because that is how people say a plan out loud. Weeks 20 to 22 multiply the repeating costs out, which is the arithmetic argument of the year. Week 24 rewrites the plan from the evidence.

Challenge Zone

A yearly plan differs by more than its numbers: it cannot be held in the head, it has to survive events, and it needs review points built in. Students who name any of those three have gone past the year.

The strands

CentsA dollar cut into a hundred parts. Decimal notation, comparing to the hundredth, and adding and subtracting with the points lined up. This is the year the book stops rounding.
The PlanAn amount, a stretch of time, named categories, and the requirement that the parts reach the whole. A plan is choices made early, and it is only a plan if it adds up.
ComparingPrice per one, worked out by dividing rather than guessed. And the harder half: the best price per one is not the best buy if it comes attached to more than you wanted.
Keeping TrackThe plan column, the actual column, and the difference between them. Nothing else in the book can tell a student something they did not already know.
Sharing OutFractions of an amount of money, equivalent fractions that come to the same dollars, and three parts that have to add to one whole.
RepeatingA cost multiplied by the times it comes back. The whole point is that the yearly number is not available to anybody who has not multiplied.
The UnplannedA cost nobody could have known about, and the set-aside that exists so the answer is not always emptying the saving.
Coming InThe other side of a plan, including the case where the amount arriving is different every time.
ReviewPulling a trimester into something a student could act on.

Four things this book does not do

Nothing is sold. There is no product, no customer and no market day anywhere in this year. That is The Venture Year, which is also Grade 4 and is a different book in a different folder. This one is about the student’s own money, and the two never meet.

Nobody is asked what anything costs at their house. Every amount is the student’s own or is printed on the page. If a child volunteers a family amount, do not write it on the board, do not compare it with anybody else’s, and move back to the page.

No plan is marked right or wrong. A plan is a set of decisions a student made about their own money. What can be marked is whether the categories add to the amount, whether the record was kept, and whether the difference column was worked out correctly. Whether $3.00 a week for snacks is too much is not a question this guide answers, and a teacher who answers it will get plans written to please them rather than plans that are true.

There is no interest, no rate and no card. Borrowing appeared once in Grade 3 and does not appear here at all. Interest is Grade 8 in the pathway and it stays there.
