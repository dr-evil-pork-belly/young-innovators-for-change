Teacher Guide

The Choosing Year · Grade 3 · 36 weeks

Answers, the point of each week, and the specific wrong turn each one produces. Written for a teacher who has never run an enterprise program and does not intend to become an expert in one.

Where a question is a judgment call this guide says so rather than inventing a correct answer to mark against. Whether a wait is worth it, or how much to give, or whether a choice was right, are all questions this book asks and does not answer.

Weeks 20 and 21 never ask a child what anything costs at their house, and the notes on those weeks say what to do if one volunteers an amount anyway. Some children in the room will be in homes where this is a hard subject, and the pages are built so that they never have to say so.

All 210 numeric answers below are recomputed from the problem as stated in the workbook by verify.py, which ships beside this file and runs on every build.

Trimester 1 · Weeks 1 to 12

You cannot have both

There is enough for one of two things. Week 2 gives the other one a box on the page and every week afterwards points back at it. Weeks 4 to 7 are the arithmetic the rest of the year runs on, early on purpose, so the choosing weeks are about the choice.

Week 1Two Things, One Coin

Choices · sits beside: Adding and subtracting within 1000 (3.NBT.2)
The point

There is enough for one of two things. Not a puzzle with a trick in it, a description of what money is like for everybody.

Answers and marking

Students who answer which one they would pick and stop. The question on this page is whether both fit, and the picking is week 2.

Challenge Zone

Two items both times, for different reasons: the first set runs out of money at three items, and so does the second. Students who notice that the same answer came from different arithmetic have the point.

Two things this book does not do

Nothing is sold. There is no product, no customer and no market day anywhere in this year. That is Grade 4, and building a gentler version of it here is the single most obvious way this book could have gone wrong. This year is about the money a student already has and what it costs them to choose.

Nobody is asked what anything costs at their house. Weeks 20 and 21 are about what a home pays for, as a list, and every number on those pages is printed on them. If a child volunteers an amount, do not write it on the board, do not compare it with anybody else's, and move back to the list.

Week 31 mentions borrowing once, at the level of you get it now and you owe it. There is no interest, no rate and no card in this book. Interest is a much later year.

A Grade 3 student could be handed this and the Grade 3 mathematics book in the same year. Both are 36 weeks, both are two pages a week. They are alternatives rather than a pair, and a school should be told so.
