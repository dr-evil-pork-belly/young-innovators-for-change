Teacher Guide

The Keeping Year · Grade 5 · 36 weeks

Answers, the point of each week, and the specific wrong turn each one produces. Written for a teacher who has never run an enterprise program and does not intend to become an expert in one.

Where a question is a judgment call this guide says so rather than inventing a correct answer to mark against. A student’s own plan is the clearest case: what can be marked is whether it adds up, and nothing else about it can.

No page asks a child what anything costs at their house. Every amount in the book is either the student’s own or is printed on the page. Some children in the room will be in homes where money is a hard subject, and the year is built so that they never have to say so to finish it.

All 239 numeric answers below are recomputed from the problem as stated in the workbook by verify.py, which ships beside this file and runs on every build.

Trimester 1 · Weeks 1 to 12

The price is not the cost

Weeks 1 to 4 put the price tag in its place: it is the bottom block of a stack and almost everybody compares bottom blocks. Weeks 5 to 7 divide the whole cost by the use you got, including the honest version on something that did not work out. Weeks 8 and 9 compare two options over the same stretch of time and find the month where the cheaper one stops being cheaper. Weeks 10 and 11 admit what the arithmetic cannot do.

Trimester 2 · Weeks 13 to 24

Money with other people

Splitting a cost evenly, then unevenly with fractions that have to reach one whole, then by use rather than by head count. Then whose the thing is when people paid different amounts. Weeks 18 to 21 are owing, from both sides of the same ledger, and week 21 asks the question most books avoid. Weeks 22 and 23 write the agreement and then break the thing, in that order on purpose.

Trimester 3 · Weeks 25 to 36

Deciding well

Decisions with more than one number in them, and the habit of naming the number that would change your mind. Then the two arrangements a fifth grader meets most, paying monthly and borrowing, both answered with week 9's crossover. Weeks 31 to 34 turn the year on the student's own life, ending with the pattern in their own decisions, which is worth more than any rule in the book.

The strands

Running CostsWhat a thing keeps costing after it is paid for, including the one nobody names: that it eventually has to be replaced.
Per UseThe whole cost divided by the times it actually got used. The number that settles arguments about whether something was expensive, and it cuts both ways.
ComparingTwo options over the same stretch of time, the month they cross, and the reasons that have no dollar amount and belong in the decision anyway.
TogetherOne cost, more than one person. Even splits, uneven splits as fractions with unlike denominators, splitting by use, and who owns what afterwards.
OwingMoney that is somebody else's and is with you, or yours and is with them. Both go in the same ledger, on the day.
DecidingDecisions with more than one number, ranked rather than scored, and the habit of naming what would change your mind.
ReviewPulling a trimester into something a student could act on.

Four things this book does not do

Nothing is sold. There is no product, no customer and no market day anywhere in this year. That is The Numbers Year, which is also Grade 5 and is a different book in a different folder. This one is about the student’s own things and their own agreements, and the two never meet.

Nobody is asked what anything costs at their house. Every amount is the student’s own or is printed on the page. If a child volunteers a family amount, do not write it on the board, do not compare it with anybody else’s, and move back to the page.

No decision is marked right or wrong. A student's own agreement, their own shares, their own old purchase costed honestly: none of those has a right answer. What can be marked is the arithmetic inside them, and there is plenty of it: whether the shares reach one whole, whether the amounts add back to the cost, whether the cost per use was divided the right way round. Whether a thing was worth buying is not a question this guide answers, and a teacher who answers it will get pages written to please them rather than pages that are true.

Nobody is charged for lending. Weeks 18 to 21 are about money owed between friends, and none of it earns anything. Nobody in this book pays back more than they borrowed, and week 21's challenge asks a student what they would do if somebody offered. Interest is Grade 8 in the pathway and it stays there.
